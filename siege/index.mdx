A two-player game played with a 54-card deck plus a spare joker of each colour, and a pair of dice. One player is **red**, the other **black**.

[**Play it →**](./play.html) — pass-and-play on one screen, or online over a room code with a friend.

## Setup

Split the deck by colour: each player takes the 24 cards of their own colour — hearts and diamonds for **red**, spades and clubs for **black**, two of every rank A–Q. The four kings and four jokers come out as foundations, not wall cards. Shuffle your 24 and deal them into a **castle**: four columns, each built bottom-up with the first row face-down, then alternating face-up and face-down. Then secretly set your two kings and two jokers, one face-down under each column. Two of your columns hide a king; two hide a joker decoy, and your opponent doesn't know which is which.

## Your turn

Choose one of the enemy's columns and **siege its top card**: roll 2d6, and you capture the card if your roll is **at least** its value (A = 1, J = 11, Q = 12). **Doubles count double** — 2,2 is 8, 6,6 is 24. Capture the card and the next one down is exposed.

Face-down cards stay hidden while you siege them — the defender only tells you whether you succeeded. The exception: if you grind **two or more columns down to the same hidden row**, those aligned face-down cards flip up for everyone to see.

## Pairs

Captured a matching pair? Once per turn you may spend it on one of two combos (the pair is used up either way, hit or miss):

- **Batter** — add the card's face value minus 3 to a roll (a pair of 10s is +7).
- **Reinforce** — drop one of the two cards face-up on top of one of your own columns, rebuilding a wall.

## Winning

You win the moment you've **fully captured both columns hiding a king** — you don't have to capture the king itself, just clear everything on top of it. The jokers are dead ends; spend your sieges on the wrong columns and you've wasted them.

## Party variant

Instead of kings and jokers, make **reward** and **punishment** cards, and draw two of each as the column foundations. Make sure they're indistinguishable from the back.
